Thesis. GPU and neocloud debt is not repricing because investors have stopped believing in AI demand. Demand is still ahead of supply: the B300 rents for $7.68/GPU-hr, up 15% in a month, and NVIDIA guides to roughly 70% growth next year.4, 5 The debt is repricing because its holders are on the wrong side of the trade. Neoclouds and their lenders have been structurally short compute: they sell the hours forward at a fixed rate while carrying the hardware, the debt and the residual. Anthropic, OpenAI, NVIDIA and the hyperscalers are long, and the value of the infrastructure is externalized to them. CoreWeave’s 2031 notes trade at 81 cents to yield 15.4%; Meta sold 2031 notes the same week at 5.85%.1, 2 Even hyperscaler-leased SPV paper is marked down: Meta’s Hyperion bonds fell from above 109 to about 92.3 Long Compute Fund I takes the other side. It finances GPUs without requiring an offtaker, prices the merchant risk accordingly, and shares in the upside a fixed-rate contract gives away.

5What the fund does

Long Compute Fund I finances GPU clusters, starting with ZODE’s, in a way that does not require an offtaker. Instead of a fixed coupon behind a take-or-pay contract, it prices the merchant risk it takes and participates in the cluster’s merchant and inference cash flow, so more of the buyer’s spread in section 4 goes to its LPs. Fast payback (section 2) and ZODE’s low basis in already-powered buildings protect the downside; the upside rises with what the hours sell for. The fund is evergreen: sites outlive GPUs, so repaid capital rolls into each new generation. Target: 2–3x for LPs from merchant compute, more from tokens.
