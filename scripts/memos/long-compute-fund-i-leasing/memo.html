Thesis. GPU and neocloud debt is not repricing because investors have stopped believing in AI demand. Demand is still ahead of supply: the B300 rents for $7.68/GPU-hr, up 15% in a month, and NVIDIA guides to roughly 70% growth next year.4, 5 The debt is repricing because its holders are on the wrong side of the trade. Neoclouds and their lenders have been structurally short compute: they sell the hours forward at a fixed rate while carrying the hardware, the debt and the residual. Anthropic, OpenAI, NVIDIA and the hyperscalers are long, and the value of the infrastructure is externalized to them. CoreWeave’s 2031 notes trade at 81 cents to yield 15.4%; Meta sold 2031 notes the same week at 5.85%.1, 2 Even hyperscaler-leased SPV paper is marked down: Meta’s Hyperion bonds fell from above 109 to about 92.3 Long Compute Fund I takes the other side: its investors own GPUs, lease them without requiring an offtaker, share in the upside a fixed-rate contract gives away, and take the depreciation.

5What the fund does

The Long Compute Fund is the umbrella for separate accounts. Each investor’s Deployment LLC buys GPUs at a ZODE site and leases them to ZODE, which runs the site, without requiring an offtaker. Base rent returns the GPU cost at {{COUPON}} over {{TERM}} months; participation rent adds {{KICKER}} of the cluster’s cash after it, so more of the buyer’s spread goes to the investor, who keeps the resale and depreciation. 10Where the depreciation goes: US and non-US deployments

Each LLC claims the depreciation on its GPUs, and the partnership agreement allocates it, with the economic losses, to the investor’s Class A interest; LCF’s contingent carry does not take {{CARRY}} of the early deductions.15 Where the GPUs run decides the schedule. In the US, they take 100% bonus depreciation in the year placed in service.16 GPUs used predominantly outside the US must use the alternative depreciation system (ADS): five-year straight line, half-year convention, no bonus.17

11Who can use the depreciation

Limit What it means for the investor
Basis and at-risk Losses are capped at the investor’s basis and amount at risk: its cash, plus recourse debt it is liable for; nonrecourse equipment debt does not count18
Passive activity Equipment leasing is a rental activity, so for individuals and closely held corporations the losses offset passive income only, carry forward, and free up on sale; widely held C corporations are not limited18
Excess business loss Noncorporate investors’ net business losses above the annual threshold carry forward as net operating losses18
Carry grant A profits interest in predictable net-lease income falls outside the usual tax-free grant safe harbor; this shapes how LCF’s carry is set up, not whether the structure works19

12How the accounts build LCF’s track record

Every account follows one investment process and one reporting standard, recording capital deployed, equipment bought and rent collected; all fees, expenses, financing and distributions; and investor returns after fees and carry, with realized proceeds kept apart from the estimated value of GPUs still held. Each management agreement lets LCF keep and use accurate, anonymized performance data. The result is an LCF managed-account track record, with investor-specific tax benefits shown separately, that a later pooled fund can present with clear attribution and disclosure of differences in strategy, fees and leverage, under the SEC marketing rule where it applies.20
