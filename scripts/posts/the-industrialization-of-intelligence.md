## Thesis

ZODE is building a new type of frontier AI lab with a singular organizing principle: to become the lowest-cost producer of tokens at industrial scale.

We are entering the second inning of AI. The first was defined by breakthroughs in intelligence. The second will be defined by the economics of producing it. As increasingly capable open models become widely available, intelligence will become cheaper, more abundant, and embedded throughout the economy. Software, autonomous agents, and physical machines will consume it continuously. At that scale, the ability to produce intelligence at the lowest cost becomes a defining competitive advantage.

We view AI less as a proprietary invention and more as the discovery of a foundational resource, like oil or electricity. Our thesis is that the intellectual property and network effects that defined the software era will not determine who wins the industrialization of intelligence. As capability becomes widely available, economic power will concentrate around the scarce resources required to produce it.

Intelligence has three essential inputs: algorithms, data, and compute. Algorithms and data are information. Once available, they can be replicated and distributed globally, and increasingly generated by machines themselves. Compute is physical. It depends on chips, manufacturing capacity, data centers, and energy. Algorithms can spread at the speed of software. Compute expands at the speed of the physical world.

This creates a fundamental asymmetry. Algorithms and data cannot produce intelligence without compute. But compute, running capable models, can increasingly generate synthetic data, discover better algorithms, and automate research itself. As this feedback loop accelerates, intelligence becomes capable of producing more of its own intellectual inputs. Every iteration still requires physical computation.

The bottleneck therefore shifts from the supply of exceptional researchers to the supply of productive compute. The capacity to build and power that compute becomes a constraint on both how much intelligence can be produced and how quickly it can improve.

ZODE's thesis is that the long-term winners in AI will be the companies that can manufacture and deliver compute most efficiently to the end customer.

In the future, compute will be the moat.

## Being Long Compute

The default view is that the advantage of Anthropic and OpenAI is having been at the frontier and having the strongest researchers. This is and has been true, but they have also been structurally long compute, whereas neoclouds and financiers have been structurally short compute.

Being long compute means buying it forward: committing to capacity years ahead and keeping everything it produces. Anthropic's IPO filing discloses [$900.9 billion of compute and infrastructure commitments](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/2130357/000162828026072484/anthropicpbc-sx1ax2.htm), 64% of the [$1.40 trillion](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/10/anthropic-sets-ipo-price-range-of-70-to-78-per-share-to-reach-an-implied-valuation-of-1-404-trillion/) it would be worth at the top of its range. OpenAI's contracts come to at least [$614 billion through 2030](https://www.investing.com/news/stock-market-news/what-are-the-ai-lab-compute-commitments-5010113) on Bernstein's conservative count, 49% of the [$1.25 trillion](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-06/openai-completes-11-billion-portion-of-funding-at-1-25-trillion-value) its latest round valued it at. A large share of what the market pays for these companies is the compute they have locked up, and the right to everything that compute produces.

Being short compute is the other side of the same trade: selling the hours forward at a fixed rate while owning the GPUs, the debt and the residual. CoreWeave has [$132.3 billion of contracted revenue](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1769628/000176962826000465/crwv-20260930.htm), almost five times its $26.9 billion market value, and $47.5 billion of debt against it. Nebius has sold [$38.7 billion](https://evidinvest.com/blog/nbis-q3-2026-read-back-contracts-capex-financing-2026-11-13) forward against a $42.4 billion market value. When a contracted hour is worth more than its contract price, the buyer keeps the difference. When it is worth less, the buyer renegotiates, and the owner and its lenders carry the loss.

Credit markets are pricing exactly this. On November 3, Meta [sold notes due 2031 at 5.85%](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1326801/000119312526448015/d135326d424b5.htm). CoreWeave's notes due the same year [trade at 82.6 cents, yielding 14.9%](https://news.kilambi.com/leverage/bond/u2069eac4/). The project debt behind data centers built for Meta, Microsoft and Oracle has [fallen to between 87 and 92 cents](https://dealroom.co/news/other-1ni755j-investors-dump-data-centre-bonds-as-ai-borrowers-face-darwin-moment/) on the dollar since July.

None of this is because demand for compute has weakened. The [B300 rental index](https://www.silicondata.com/products/silicon-index/b300) rose 13% in the last month, to $7.67 a GPU-hour. The asset is getting more valuable. The parties who sold its output forward don't share in the increase.


A contract doesn't take its owner out of the compute market, either. Take-or-pay contracts are mostly signed with resellers and labs whose own revenue moves with the spot price, and who renegotiate exactly when it falls. The GPUs that secure the debt resell at the same spot price that caused the default. In the stress that matters, the price, the buyer's credit and the collateral fall together. The short side kept the downside and gave away the upside.

The lesson isn't that infrastructure is a bad business. It's that the value of compute has accrued to whoever is long it. ZODE intends to own the infrastructure and stay long the compute it produces.

## Recursive Self-Improvement

The second assumption we reject is that the software layer of compute, the inference engines, kernel tuning and orchestration, is a durable moat for the companies that build it.

In October, DeepSeek open-sourced [DeepInfer](https://github.com/deepseek-ai/DeepInfer), the inference engine behind its own service, where [a single cluster serves 155 trillion tokens a day](https://github.com/deepseek-ai/DeepInfer/blob/main/doc%2Finference_deployment_qa.md) at $38 of revenue per GPU-hour off-peak. Within days, the SGLang team had [merged DeepSeek's kernels and matched or beaten DeepInfer on its own benchmarks](https://www.lmsys.org/blog/2026-10-24-dsv41-deepinfer/), with the KV cache, disaggregation and load balancing DeepSeek had held back. The best inference stack for a leading open model is now free, and it ships with the model.

The tuning that's left is increasingly done by AI. Meta's [KernelEvolve](https://engineering.fb.com/2026/04/02/developer-tools/kernelevolve-how-metas-ranking-engineer-agent-optimizes-ai-infrastructure/) agent raised inference throughput on a production ads model by more than 60% over hand-optimized kernels, in hours of automated search rather than weeks of expert work. In the MLSys 2026 FlashInfer contest, a Fused MoE kernel [written entirely by coding agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.14560), with humans only setting the workflow and enforcing correctness, ran 1.71× faster than the FlashInfer baseline and beat the best agent-assisted entry.

Post-training is following. On the open [Athanor](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.21055) system, four Claude Opus 5.5 agents given no research advice spent 104 hours post-training Qwen3-4B-Base, and the result matched or beat the official Qwen3-4B-Instruct on both its development and held-out suites. Anthropic's [automated alignment researchers](https://alignment.anthropic.com/2026/automated-alignment-researchers/) found methods that outperformed the best ideas of 28 experienced human researchers, on average within about six hours of search.

Every iteration of that loop runs on compute. As AI does more of the work of improving AI, the scarce input stops being the engineers and becomes the machines they run on.

That is a problem for much of the inference and neocloud market. Many of these companies were fueled with excessive venture money, moved out on the risk curve, and built bloated organizations. It doesn't make sense to have a team of 50 to 500 engineers building orchestration for a neocloud or tuning Chinese open models when AI will be able to do that easily, and open-source labs are already giving it away. That is not a moat. Most of these companies don't own their compute, and most can't finance more of it.

## Principles

Our approach rests on three mutually reinforcing principles:

1. **Vertical integration.** Owning the critical elements of compute production: the power, land, data center, GPUs and inference distribution.
2. **Power optimization.** Modulating power to each rack with intelligent power routing, in order to maximize the amount of productive compute derived from a fixed electrical footprint.
3. **Merchant compute.** Rather than relying on long-term contracted GPU leases, going maximally long compute, through a combination of contracted capacity, merchant GPU markets, and direct inference services.

Open source will continue to compress margins and take a growing share of total token spend from the frontier labs. Further advances in intelligence will continue to make the neoclouds' software a commodity, leaving the cost of producing compute as the margin that lasts.

## Things to Prove

The plan is a sequence. Each stage has to work before the next is worth funding.

```chart
roadmap
```

### a. The first megawatt

Secure our first data center capacity and sell its output two ways: merchant GPU-hours and direct inference.

What would need to be true:

- Merchant rates hold well above our cost of producing a GPU-hour, without a long-term contract underneath.
- Inference on open models earns more per GPU-hour than renting the GPU out. DeepSeek reports $38 per GPU-hour off-peak on its own service.
- We can keep the cluster busy without a single anchor customer.

### b. Our first site: 30 MW

Buy and build our first data center with ZODE modules.

What would need to be true:

- Powered land can be bought for less than leasing colocation costs over a GPU's life.
- ZODE modules deploy faster and for less per megawatt than a conventional build.
- Power routing delivers measurably more productive compute from the same electrical footprint.
- The first megawatt's cash flow and track record make the GPUs financeable without an offtaker.

### c. Production: 100 MW and up

Turn the build into a repeatable production line across sites.

What would need to be true:

- Each new site repeats the last: the same modules and power design, at a falling cost per megawatt.
- Merchant and inference demand absorbs 100 MW without our selling it forward at fixed rates.
- Capital comes back fast enough that each generation of GPUs finances the next.

### d. The full stack: 1 GW and up

Build our own models, harness and wafers.

What would need to be true:

- Open models stay close enough to the frontier that owning the cheapest tokens beats owning the best model.
- At gigawatt scale, our own models and serving harness earn more per megawatt than serving others'.
- Our volume justifies custom silicon, and we can secure the wafer supply to build it.
